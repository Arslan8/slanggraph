| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| Cancellation lifecycle | `MacOSSandboxExecutor._invoke`, native guardian, and graph task cancellation | In three targeted trials an authorized Alice read completed and the internal route accepted its result, but the canceled graph caller received none; identity-checked worker and guardian exited | An already authorized resource operation need not roll back when cancellation arrives; controller and process observations are trusted |

The compiled Lean core checks the model predicates over encoded grants, obligations, activation events and cleanup certificate. `ResourceGrantCoverage.lean` proves exact generated resource-obligation inventory and service guards; `ReceiptCoverage.lean` checks assignment/evidence coverage and binding; `WorkerRoute.lean` proves that `GO` follows matching activation and success follows confirmed cleanup. These properties do not prove macOS kernel behavior, Cedar policy correctness, Python controller integrity, arbitrary application node confinement, or the accuracy of the controller's observations. Live mutation cases in `security-final-03` exercise the actual call path. Certificate replay in `verify.py` checks retained traces separately and cannot establish that an unrecorded call passed the live gate. See each trial's `evidence.json` and `resource-events.jsonl` for the receiving phase and independent resource observation.

The 45-case common mapping compares the **requested property and forbidden effect**, not the internal host mechanism. Linux may enforce a requirement that macOS refuses; both can preserve the manifest policy if the refusal is prior to execution. The macOS-only checker/preflight and supplemental cases stay outside that comparison until a Linux counterpart matches the attack and oracle. `SUPPLEMENTAL_DISPOSITIONS.md` records observations that are qualified or inconclusive rather than promoted to proved properties.
